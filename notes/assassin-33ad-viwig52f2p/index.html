The feature presentation

The Film

Assassin 33 A.D. (2020, Timed Out Productions) follows Ram Goldstein, a Jewish atheist physicist who works at a lab owned by the industrialist Ahmed Akbar. Ram means to build a matter transporter and builds a time machine by mistake. Ahmed, a Muslim extremist, sends a hit squad to 33 A.D. to stop the Resurrection so that Jesus is remembered "as a great prophet, but not the son of God." Ram and his friends go back to stop them, across several timelines that never fully close.

In one timeline, Brandt, a soldier who blames God for the death of his family, shoots Jesus in Gethsemane. In another, the future without Christianity is a ruined city that the film calls "a world without forgiveness." In a third, there is an Antichrist cloned from Jesus' DNA. The film opened in a small number of theaters on January 24, 2020, then went to Prime Video. In 2021 the director released a recut, Black Easter, which he pitched as "less offensive to Muslims."

The guest is Jordan Collver, a comics writer who used to be an evangelical creationist. Between the panel segments, Ti plays his phone interview with the writer-director, Jim Carroll.

What the director said

  • He renamed Resurrection Time Conspiracy to a title that starts with "A" because those sort higher in video listings.
  • Time "has to override itself, just like a hard drive," so a change to the past takes time to reach the present. That gives the heroes a deadline.
  • He tried an atheist villain first, but "you just couldn't get the incentive." Muslim extremists had a motive: keep Jesus a prophet and show that the Resurrection was a myth.
  • About 80 zero-star reviews arrived the day before release. The worst hate mail came from "the self-righteous, hypocritical, judgmental Christians."
  • The cast's Arab characters are played mostly by Hispanic actors. He says the casting director picked the candidates.
  • A sequel depends on whether the film makes money. "If not, then this is my last guess at making motion pictures."

What the panel said

  • Andrew hated the film until he heard the interview: "I can see where his worldview fits."
  • Tushna calls it a film of two halves: earnest faith film and accidental Life of Brian.
  • Jordan, the former creationist, sees a sincere believer "writing what he knows," and likes that Ram's conversion is not forced.
  • The panel finds the racial stereotypes worse than the religious ones. One line shocked Tushna: that only atheists would want to erase Jesus outright.
  • Tushna explains real quantum teleportation (you move properties, not atoms) and the no-cloning theorem. The panel plans a time-travel episode.

Dallas entrepreneur. Founder of Champion National Security and of Marriage Boot Camp, which he hosted with his wife Elizabeth for 7 seasons on WE tv.

  • The Marriage Boot Camp website (2020) says its founder Jim Carroll "wrote and produced the thriller, Evil Behind You and Assassin 33 AD."

The Life

1954

Born in Dallas

Son of an engineer and a school teacher. At Garland High School he was the backup quarterback and the school chess champion.

1973

Born again at a speed trap

On New Year's Eve he was pulled over for speeding next to Old Orchard Baptist Church. He walked into its all-night prayer chapel, "had an encounter with God," and "accepted Christ as his personal savior."

1976

Flips the tables on Madalyn Murray O'Hair

At a debate with America's most famous atheist, he prayed, opened his Bible to Jesus and the money changers, and overturned the beer and book tables at what he called a "fake church." He also let the air out of 80 tires. The misdemeanor ended his plan to join the FBI or Secret Service. He reads it as providence: "God had better plans."

1977

Private investigator, then bounty hunter

He worked for the Texas investigator William C. Dear, then caught bail jumpers on his own. Then he quit to sell Purina.

1980

Champion National Security

"Before even applying for a license, God opened doors." The company grew to more than 3,000 guards. He sold it in 2020.

1986

The seminar that changed him

He went to the "YOU" seminar, later called Pathways, run by Dr. Phil McGraw and Thelma Box. "Dr. Phil broke through the anger." Then he trained under Dr. Phil.

1990

Cover of Darkness

An "end-time thriller" from the Christian publisher Huntington House.

1992

Angel Vision

A novel "about people trapped in the spiritual realm," from the same publisher.

1994

The Road Adventure seminar

His own weekend seminar, started "with Dr. Phil's encouragement." It became the nonprofit Marriage Boot Camp in 2002.

2001

Hollywood Dating Blunders

Dating advice from five years of teaching a singles Sunday school class.

2006

Evil Behind You

His first film, a low-budget Christian thriller made mostly with college students. It also has Islamic terrorists.

2012

Reality TV

Marriage Boot Camp on WE tv, first with Bridezillas, then with reality stars. Off camera, the hosts offered to lead the cast to Christ.

2020

Assassin 33 A.D.

Written, directed and produced by Jim Carroll. Jesus is played by the American Idol singer Jason Castro.

2021

Black Easter

The director's cut. "Less offensive to Muslims," with "better special effects" and "less cheesy," in his own pitch.

2025

The Pickleball Killer

An unproduced script about an elderly ex-con with superhuman speed who fights a cartel. More than 225 contest wins, his site says.

THE THEOLOGY

No church or denomination is on record for him today. But his conversion story, his novels, his seminars and his interviews agree. Each belief below has a source, and each one is in the film.

TENET 01

Born again, Baptist style

A personal conversion on a specific night (December 31, 1973) is the center of his story.

ON SCREENThe skeptic Ram moves slowly toward faith, and Amy, the one firm believer, is "very non-judgmental."
TENET 02

Forgiveness is what Christianity gave the world

He contrasts Christ's "seventy times seven" with "the Jewish law was an eye for an eye." He adds that "forgiveness really is a gift for yourself anyway."

ON SCREENThe timeline without Christianity is "a world without forgiveness." Brandt is forgiven on the cross.
TENET 03

Good psychology is good theology

His faith went through Dr. Phil's seminar, and his own seminars teach people to release "anger, bitterness and resentment." Marriage Boot Camp calls itself "a therapeutic, healing ministry not an evangelical ministry."

ON SCREENBrandt is the seminar's typical case: a believer whom grief makes "bitter, angry, mean." Carroll wanted that arc "realistic, instead of" the "sappy" faith-film version.
TENET 04

Faith without proof

"I don't think you can prove Jesus is God, and I don't think you can prove Jesus isn't God." Most resurrection films try to argue from evidence. He does not.

ON SCREENThe body of Jesus leaves the tomb in a flash that looks like the teleporter's, so that Ram can still doubt.
TENET 05

God has no time factor

"If God wants to be in the future right now, he can." That is why time travel did not seem a problem to him "on sci-fi using God."

ON SCREENHistory can be changed, but it always returns to the Gospel account.
TENET 06

Jesus: human in flesh, fluent in English

Jesus "doesn't know exactly what's going on because that's being covered from him." But "if Jesus is God of all people, he can speak every language." His friends argued with him about the first part.

ON SCREENSimon tells Jesus about The Passion of the Christ, and Jesus does not know what a movie is.
TENET 07

End times and the spirit world

He wrote two Christian novels in the early 1990s, an end-times thriller and a story of people "trapped in the spiritual realm."

ON SCREENAn apocalypse timeline, an Antichrist cloned from Jesus' DNA, and a sequel tease. He cut an apocalypse sequence that would have cost about $1.4M more.
TENET 08

The atheist as the enemy of religion

In 1976 he overturned tables at Madalyn Murray O'Hair's event. In 2020 he said that only an atheist would want to erase Jesus outright.

ON SCREENThe atheist villain was dropped for lack of "incentive," and Muslim extremists got the part. The hero is still an atheist, but a likeable one.
TENET 09

Against the judgmental church

His seminar welcomes "every faith" and unmarried couples, and its leaders say that excluding people is "not the way Jesus worked." He calls his harshest critics "self-righteous, hypocritical, judgmental Christians."

ON SCREENFaith films should be fun and not preach "to the choir." The film puts a Christian behind the gun that shoots Jesus.
TENET 10

Sunday-school literalism

He reads the Gospels the way a Bible-study class does, verse by verse, and looks for gaps that a story can fill.

ON SCREENMark 14:51, the young man who runs away naked, becomes Ram in a lab coat. Simon of Cyrene and Barabbas become time travelers.

"Muslims wouldn't do this. Only extremists would do this."// the line he says should have prevented the backlash

The controversy

The Villains

Carroll's reasoning was about story mechanics. In his reading, Islam honors Jesus as a prophet and holds that the disciples "created the resurrection myth." So a Muslim extremist has a motive to stop the Resurrection and keep Jesus as a prophet. The villain's parents are Muslim converts to Christianity whom extremists killed. A line of dialogue puts his hatred down to Stockholm syndrome.

He expected anger from Christians and was surprised by the anger from Muslims. Reviewers called the villain a stereotype, and the podcast panel found the racial casting worse than the religious framing. A year later his recut was "less offensive to Muslims," with a voiceover that tells viewers the villain is "an extremist."

The twist ending

The Verdict

Assassin 33 A.D. is not the work of a Christian-right ideologue, or of a cynic who saw a market. It comes from a Dallas businessman who was born again at a speed trap in 1973, fought atheism in person in 1976, wrote end-times fiction for a Christian press, and found his life's work in Dr. Phil's anger-release seminar.

His theology is therapeutic evangelicalism. The cross is the source of forgiveness, forgiveness heals bitterness, faith is a choice that evidence cannot force, and judgmental believers are as much the problem as extremists. A God outside time makes the premise safe for him. The Muslim villains come from a plot need, chosen for motive, with no thought for how they would look on screen.
