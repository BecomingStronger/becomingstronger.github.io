Why this route

water_drop
Water decides the route.

The only taps are at Pine Ridge and Dry Fork, both in the first 3.3 miles. Past them, the side creeks are not reliable: on Oct 4, 2026 a hiker found every Moon Loop crossing dry. Cedar Creek is the largest stream on the trail and the one most likely to run, so every night is next to it.

camping
Rutherford Bridge is the hub.

The Southern Loop, the Smith Creek Loop and the Moon Loop connector all meet at the bridge, so one camp reaches the whole north half of the system. The bridge itself stands on private land. National Forest land starts 0.15 mi east on the trail toward the overlook, so camp there and walk back down to the creek for water.

route
Smith Creek both ways.1 and 2 nights

Day 1 comes in on the south side of the Smith Creek Loop, past the bluffs. The last day goes out on the north side through Boydsville, so you see the whole loop. The cost: the 5.7 miles from Pine Ridge past Dry Fork are walked twice, because the tap water is at that end.

hiking
Moon Loop as a day hike.2 and 3 nights

On the 2-night and 3-night plans, Day 2 is Moon Loop. It hangs off the end of a 3.6-mile connector, so any route that includes it walks the connector twice. Doing it from camp with day packs makes the repeat light, and it keeps the full-pack days to 8 and 9 miles.

signpost
Devil's Backbone as an evening walk.2 nights only

On the 2-night plan the ridge is 1.8 miles from camp, short enough for the first evening without packs. The last half mile is on Backbone Road, past the point where the trail turns away.

map
The west half is optional.

The Forest Service and a trip report both describe the west side of the Southern Loop as mostly gravel road and cattle pasture, so only the 3-night plan uses it. Its third night is on Cedar Creek again, at the Burnett School Road bridge.
